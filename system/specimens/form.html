Form

How fields are laid out, say what they need, show what's wrong and save (D134, I25; the F-rules of the forms research). A form done well: every form saves the same way, with its Save, in a dialog, a sheet or on a page; Save is never off; leaving with changes asks once. Every example below works: type, leave fields, press Save.

A create in a dialog

Up to about five fields: a dialog, its Save in the footer. One column; labels above; the minority marked in words (here “(optional)”, most fields being required); nothing red until a field is left after a change; once in error, checked as you type. Save is never off: pressed with something missing, it says what. Two or more errors, or one out of sight, get a summary at the top that links to each.

Sections, rows and widths

Sections are fieldsets with a heading. Every line ends at the form's edge (D135): a field alone on its line stretches, whatever its answer. Short fields that belong together share a row, one of them stretching and the others as wide as their answers (data-width 4, 8, 12, 20 or 32 characters); on phones the row stacks and each takes the full width.

Settings: each section has its Save

Settings save the way every form does: change what you need, then the section's Save, switches included, so nothing changes by brushing past it. Most settings are optional, so the few that are needed say “(required)”.

Rules

  • Every line ends at the form's edge: a field alone on its line stretches; a short width only beside a field that stretches. Fields sharing a row have no hint of their own (it would push their control out of line): say it in the section's hint.
  • Every field has a visible label above it; a placeholder is never a label; a hint goes between the label and the field.
  • Mark the minority in words: “(optional)” where most are required, “(required)” where most are optional; a form where everything is required says nothing.
  • Error words say what to do, in the label's words: “Enter company name”, “ZIP isn't in the right format”; never “Invalid” or “This field is required”.
  • Every form saves the same way, with its Save; nothing saves by itself. Save is never disabled to say what's missing. Off only while saving, with “Saving…”.
  • Leaving with unsaved changes asks once: Keep editing, Discard, Save. Nothing changed, nothing asked.
  • Where a form lives: a dialog up to about five fields; a side sheet for a related record; the page or the record's window for documents with money; on phones a full-screen sheet.
  • Everything typed is kept when something goes wrong.
