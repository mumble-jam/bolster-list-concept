        About this concept

        What it does

        • Bolton doesn't talk here. He stands in his vest in the banner and reacts with his face: finish a step and he winks and nods, finish everything and he laughs. No bubble, no moving mouth (your call, 26 Oct); his chat box is where you talk to him.
        • The banner lines up with the cards. The page goes on into a grid of cards, so the banner starts on the grid's left edge: Bolton on the left, standing on the banner's foot (your call, 26 Oct), then the title, one line of why and the chat box. The eye runs down one line from the title into the first card. The page is the focused width (D106), one column of work.
        • Left or centred (Banner alignment, in this panel): every banner below comes both ways. Left-aligned, Bolton stands on the left, at the start of the line the eye reads along, and the text and box follow him; centred, he stands on top of one axis with the title, the line of why and the box under him.
        • No Bolton: only the title, the line of why and the chat box, no figure and no head in the box, to see what he adds.
        • Five banners to compare (Banner, in this panel). Pick, the one the research points to, built on one order of importance: 1, the page's name, big enough to say where you are but a step below a dashboard's title, because the cards are the page's real content; 2, the one thing to do, the chat box, which carries the banner's only colour (his gold edge); 3, Bolton, smaller, a companion beside the words and not the headline; 4, the line of why, quiet. No card around it (Klaviyo, Cloaked and Shopify start straight on the page), so the step cards are the only cards and the eye runs from the title's edge into the first one. In a card: the banner as a raised card, Bolton standing on its foot. No card: the same parts straight on the page, so the cards under it are the only cards; Bolton stands on a hairline. In the box: no figure at all; Bolton's head sits inside his chat box, at its start, the way an assistant's mark sits in its input, and the box grows to be the banner's one thing. Point at his head and he looks around.
        • The research (26 Oct, second pass). The first pass looked at full-screen welcome screens with one action (Duolingo, Deputy, Hootsuite), which don't fit a banner followed by many cards. Setup pages that do have cards under the header on Mobbin: Klaviyo and Cloaked (the title left, on the cards' edge, the cards right under it), Shopify (a greeting with the assistant's box under it, left, then the setup cards), Pipedrive and Slite (text left, the picture to one side on the right), SchoolAI (the banner holds the cards). Centred headers only show up where one narrow column follows them: Google Workspace and Cake, a checklist under a centred greeting.
        • A colour per step, as Bolster does it. Point at a step and it takes a feedback tone, the way Bolster's own Getting started does (your screens, 27 Oct): the tone's surface on the card, an edge in its colour, and the icon white in a solid well of it. Estimate is info, proposal success and labor rates danger, as in Bolster; the other steps spread over the same tones so neighbours differ.
        • What a step opens. As in Bolster (your screens, 27 Oct): click a step and it opens what does it. Labor rates open a dialog right here: AutoCost's defaults for your area, each crew renamable and repriced; saving ticks the step. Steps done elsewhere in Bolster open a guide in the corner, the steps one by one with a count, and a coach mark at Jobs in the menu (Step 1 of N); the guide's first step opens the Pipeline. The other steps aren't drawn yet: send what Bolster opens for them.
        • Card shadows. The cards here take the system's raised shadow, as Elevation draws it: one soft lift, without the card body's darker bottom edge and top line (your call, 27 Oct). The system's cards draw that body, so if this holds it's a change for the system's card, with a D-entry.
        • Done cards sit into the page. A done step drops the card's lift: the system's sunken surface, no shadow, a quiet darker hover and no colour (your call, 27 Oct), so done reads at a glance down the grid. The to-do cards keep the system card's body and shadow.
        • His suggestions in the box. Press Bolton's head in the chat box and the chat's own "Try asking" questions show under it, no card, as wide as the box; press his head again to hide them. Picking one, or sending from the box, opens the chat with it already in the chat's box while he waves; it goes once he has settled (no chat on the page itself, your call, 27 Oct).
        • No ticks on the cards (your call, 27 Oct). A step is done when you've done it (saving labor rates ticks that one); done steps fold into Completed, and each has an undo in its corner ("Not done yet") that puts it back on the list. Start over, in this panel, puts the sample account back.
        • A drawing per step that tells what happens (your pick, 28 Oct, over the screen pictures, which read as wireframes). Left to right: labor rates, a crew's hourly cost being set; the estimate filling in item by item to a total; the proposal gliding to your client, who signs; spreadsheet rows becoming jobs; your details checked, then a payment landing; a sent form landing in the pipeline; your apps kept in step with Bolster; an invitation going out and your crew joining. At rest it shows how it ends; point at the card (to do, done or hidden) and it plays once, slow and eased, each part in turn. Drawn like the icons, in one line colour. The time is in the heading.
        • Actions you can skip can be hidden (feedback, 27 Oct): integrations and your team have a × in the corner. Hidden actions fold away under the grid and each comes back on its own with Show again. Two ways to fold them (Hidden actions, in this panel): one fold for all, or one fold each. On the page they're actions, not steps (your call, 29 Oct): there's no order to follow.
        • Completed, then All set, then Hidden actions (feedback, 30 Oct: not two folds one after the other). Completed folds under the grid; All set is the way out; Hidden actions sit last and quiet, under All set, there for whoever looks for them. Hiding an action shows the system's toast with Undo, for when it was a slip. All set comes last.
        • The chat box, two ways (Chat box, in this panel): an ink outline, or raised off the page with the system's popover shadow. No ring while you type in it; the mic stays gold, on the dark chat too.
        • The guide gets out of the way. Going where it points (its first step, or Jobs in the menu) puts the guide and its coach mark away; a click anywhere else puts the coach mark away.
        • Any order. Every step can be done whenever it suits you, so they sit side by side with no numbers or arrows. Bolton still suggests one to start with; the proposal waits for an estimate, because it's sent from one.
        • Finishing is its own thing. "Complete onboarding and hide Getting started" isn't a setup step: it's the way out, at the bottom.
