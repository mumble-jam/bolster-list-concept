Colour

A palette underneath and roles on top: components ask for what a colour is for (a surface, a line, an action, a status), never for a shade. Light is warm, dark is cool, and every text pair passes AA in both.

Stabletokens.cssbase.csscomponents/badge.cssdocs/decisions.mdUpdated 2026-11-05

Every colour in the product comes from system/tokens.css, in two layers. The primitives are the palette: what a colour is. The semantic tokens are the API: what it's for. A component asks for --color-surface-default or --color-status-booked-content, never for --color-cement-200, so the palette can change under it without touching the component.

Two layers

  • Primitives are ramps named after Bolster's brand colours (cement, blue print, pitch black) and the system's hues (deep red, orange, yellow, matcha, green, blue, purple), stepped 100 to 950. Five night steps exist only for the dark theme.
  • Semantic tokens are read as --color-<category>-<role>[-<state>]: surface, content, line, action, feedback, status, control, avatar. Each one is written once with both its light and its dark value.
  • Components use semantic tokens only. A primitive in system/components/ is a bug, and so is a colour from anywhere else: the product's Tailwind stock palette and its local overrides are retired (D19).
/* primitive: what it is */
--color-cement-200: #f7f5ef;
/* semantic: what it's for, light then dark */
--color-surface-canvas: light-dark(var(--color-cement-200), var(--color-pitch-black-950));

/* a component reads the role */
.card { background: var(--color-surface-default); border-color: var(--color-line-card); }

Warm light, cool dark

The two themes have different temperatures on purpose (D33). Light is warm: cement neutrals on a paper canvas, white cards with cement edges, cement hovers and tints, with Bolster's ink for text and inputs. Dark is cool: Bolster's pitch-black greys. In both, selection stays blue and the top bar and rail stay dark.

The dark theme is a first pass: every pair is contrast-checked, but it hasn't been designed in Figma yet (D14, proposed). Bolster has no dark theme today.

How the theme is picked

  • Each semantic colour is written once with light-dark(light, dark). The page's color-scheme picks the value, so nothing is duplicated per theme.
  • By default the root says color-scheme: light dark and the operating system decides. data-theme="light" or "dark" on <html> forces one.
  • color-scheme works on any element, so a part can take the other theme's colours. The top bar's search field does: the bar is dark in both themes, so the field sets color-scheme: dark and gets the dark focus ring.
  • Shadows can't be written with light-dark(), so they're set again under prefers-color-scheme: dark and [data-theme="dark"]. See Elevation and layers.
<html>                     <!-- follows the system -->
<html data-theme="dark">   <!-- always dark -->
<html data-theme="light">  <!-- always light -->

Surface

Backgrounds, from the page up. Each surface has a job; pick it by what the thing is, not by how light it looks.

  • Canvas is the page: paper in light, near black in dark. The canvas grid is drawn on it (Elevation).
  • Default is cards, the table and fields. Raised is what floats: menus, popovers, dialogs, the action bar. Both are white in light; in dark, raised is a step lighter, since a shadow hardly shows on black.
  • Sunken is a track things sit in (the segmented control, the pinned scrollbar). Tint is a quiet fill (a pressed preset, an empty state's icon). Header is the table card's header row and an active chip; group a group's row.
  • Hover has four strengths, by what it sits on: hover on the page, hover-control on white, hover-raised in menus and popovers, sidebar-hover in the sidebar.
  • Selected is a blue tint for what's picked (a ticked row, a day in a range). Current is the row whose record is open beside the list: a cement tint, since it isn't picked.
  • Chrome is the top bar, the rail, the phone's tab bar and the job workspace's header: dark in both themes. Inverse flips with the theme: toasts, the floating bulk bar, a range's ends. Scrim is behind dialogs and sheets, warm in light.

Content

Text and icons. Three levels for everything on the page's own surfaces, and one pair for each filled thing that carries text.

  • Primary, secondary, tertiary all clear 4.5:1 on white, the canvas, the sidebar and a selected row, in both themes (D11). Tertiary is no lighter than that: below it, text stops being readable, and a hint people can't read is no hint.
  • Disabled is the one level exempt from contrast, as WCAG allows. Say why something is disabled in words nearby.
  • On a fill: on-primary (highlighter yellow on the black button; black on yellow in dark), on-brand, on-accent (white on blue and red), on-chrome and on-chrome-muted on the dark bars, on-avatar, inverse on the inverse surface.
  • Link is blue print, underlined. Section is a menu's section label. highlight-search marks the matched letters of a search hit in Bolster's highlighter yellow.

Action

What can be pressed. The roles are the button's, plus blue for what's picked.

  • Primary is Bolster's black with highlighter text; in dark it flips to the yellow with black text (D4).
  • Brand is the level yellow, for the single step that moves a job forward. On the inverse surface it takes brand-on-inverse, so a toast's Undo stays readable when the toast flips light in dark (D125).
  • Accent is blue: links, selection, the selected filter card, count badges (D21). White on it passes in both themes.
  • Secondary and ghost are cement in light and the greys in dark. Danger is deep red, for what can't be undone.

Job status

A job's place in its lifecycle: lead, draft, pending, booked, in progress, active, outstanding (D12). Each status has a surface, a text colour and a dot; all pairs pass 4.5:1 in both themes.

  • Badges carry no dot (D61): the word is the cue, colour the second. The dot colour is for summary cards and for the marks.
  • Marks draw the status as a shape: a job fills a quarter at a time as it moves on, and a slash is the client saying no. They're the job list's own (D49); every other list shows a stage as its colour alone.
  • Once a shape carries the status it's a graphic, held to 3:1. Draft's, pending's and in progress's dots don't reach it, so their marks take a darker -mark colour.
  • In progress is the blue family, not blue print, so it doesn't read as selection, nor as a second shade of booked's green.

Contrast

Every text pair passes WCAG AA, 4.5:1, in light and in dark; every edge that's the only sign of a control (a field's border, the focus ring, a checked box, a status mark) passes 3:1 (D11).

Run npm run contrast after any token change. It resolves every var(), light-dark() and color-mix() in tokens.css, measures each pair the components use in both themes, and fails if one falls short. A new pair of colours goes into scripts/check-contrast.mjs in the same change that makes it.

Colour carries meaning

The product is already full of colour codes: statuses, kinds, stages. Each colour keeps one job, so people can learn it.

  • Blue is selection, focus and links. Not a button colour, not a brand colour on the page (D4, D21).
  • Yellow is Bolster's. The brand action, the highlighter text on the black button, a search's matched letters. Rare on purpose.
  • Status colours are the job's lifecycle. Feedback colours are how something went. Don't borrow one for the other.
  • No colour per job (D100, trying out). A colour for each job is a code nobody would learn; jobs are told apart by their names. Job work on the calendar and the timeline is one quiet tint for every job.
  • Where colour says what kind, it's a key. On the calendar, job work is green, sales appointments blue and events purple, the avatar hues, each with its swatch in Show (D101, trying out).
  • Never colour alone. A badge has its word, a mark its shape, an error its message.

What's still open

  • The dark theme is contrast-checked but not yet designed in Figma (D14, proposed).
  • Status colours are the job's lifecycle; invoices, estimates, vendor orders and tasks borrow them case by case, with no written map from each thing's statuses to a colour.
  • No palette for charts: the summary cards' graphs reuse status colours, and a dashboard would need categorical and sequential scales.
  • Raspberry, teal and cyan have three or four steps and purple has no 950: fine while nothing semantic uses them, a gap the day something does.
  • In dark, tertiary text and the accent on the pitch-black 900 surfaces (a table's header, a pointed-at menu row, an open chip, a form's “(optional)” in a dialog) come to 4.3–4.4:1, just under AA; npm run contrast doesn't check those pairs.
