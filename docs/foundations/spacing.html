Spacing and layout

Everything sits on a 4px grid: the spaces between things, the heights of controls, the lines of text. The app shell sets the frame, three content widths set the measure, and the page itself is the one thing that scrolls.

Stabletokens.csscomponents/shell.csspatterns/list-page.cssUpdated 2026-11-02

Spacing comes from one scale of 4px steps, and so do the heights of controls and the line heights of text. Because everything is a multiple of the same unit, a toolbar's controls line up, text sits on the same rhythm as the boxes around it, and nobody has to choose between 6, 7 and 8.

The 4px grid

  • Every layout value is a multiple of 4 (D2): padding, gaps, margins, widths. The scale runs from --space-1 (4) to --space-20 (80).
  • 2px is for optical nudges inside a control (the segmented control's inset, a 2px gap between its options), never for layout. 1px is for hairlines only.
  • Pick by relation. 4 inside compact controls, 8 between items in a group, 12 to 24 between groups and inside cards and sections, 24 around the page's content (16 on phones).
  • The product used a 2px grid, with 6, 10 and 14 among its most common values. They move to the nearest step that says the same thing: 6 to 4 or 8, 10 to 8 or 12, 14 to 12 or 16.

Control heights

Buttons, fields and segmented controls share one height scale, so controls in a row line up without anyone matching them by hand (D5).

  • Small in toolbars, tables and dense headers. Medium, the default, in forms, dialogs and page headers. Large for a form's main action on a phone.
  • 56 is the button's extra large, kept for the field app's one action a crew lead taps with gloves on. It isn't a control height of its own.
  • The product's small buttons ranged from 18 to 62px tall, and its fields were 48 beside 40px selects.

Touch targets

On a touch screen (pointer: coarse) every target is at least 44px: --touch-target-min. A small button keeps its 32px drawing and gets a 44px hit area around it; menu rows, popover options and checkbox and radio rows grow to 44. On phones, fields also set their text at 16px, so iOS doesn't zoom into them.

Content widths

The page's content is centred inside the shell, up to a width picked by what the page holds. Set it with data-width on .app-content.

Breakpoints

The shell changes shape at two widths, and the list page adds two more for the record beside it (D15).

WidthShellAlso
Under 768Top bar and a bottom tab bar; no rail. More opens every section.Popovers and menus open as bottom sheets. Content padding is 16. Create floats above the tab bar.
768 to 1023Top bar and the rail. The section sidebar opens over the page from the top bar.
1024 and upTop bar, rail and the section sidebar beside the page.
1280 and upA list's record opens beside it; below 1280 it's a side sheet.
1280 to 1439Beside an open record, the list keeps only name, status and amount.
  • The widths are Tailwind's, so the React prototype's responsive utilities agree with the system's CSS.
  • They're written in media queries, not as tokens: CSS can't read a custom property in a media query.
  • The product kept its sidebar open at 768 and squeezed forms to 488px with three-line labels; at that width the sidebar now opens over the page instead.

The shell

The app shell's measurements match the product's (D24), with two changes: the section sidebar fits its longest item plus 48, so it's 256 rather than 300 (D26), and the rail widens over the page when pointed at (D25). The page header is as tall as the sidebar's header, so the rules under them meet in one line. See App shell.

The page is the one scroller

A page scrolls down, never a card or a part of one; headers stick to the page under the bars (at --page-top) and slide up with them; a part wider than the screen scrolls sideways only, with the card's own scrollbar when it's in a card and one pinned to the window's foot (.page-hscroll) when it sits on the page, the timeline the one exception (D141). Only what floats over the page scrolls inside itself (D128). The whole contract is in Page scrolling.

Usage

  • Use the tokens by name (var(--space-4)), never the pixel value, so a reader sees the step.
  • A page passes only what's its own: where its content starts, which width it takes. The shell's frame, padding and scrolling come from the system.

What's still open

  • Breakpoints are written in media queries and prose, not as tokens (CSS can't use a custom property in a media query): a custom-media or build step would make them one source.
  • No layout grid for dashboards and forms (columns, gutters, how cards span).
  • Density is settled per component (the pipeline's card size) but not as a system-wide choice.
  • The list page breaks at 1280 and 1440 (the record beside the list), which D15 doesn't list, and D15's 640 isn't used by any system CSS. D15 also gives the section sidebar as 300; the token is 256 (D26).
