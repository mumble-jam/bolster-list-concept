Foundations

Typography

Cera Pro for the interface, Space Grotesk for titles and big figures, eleven type roles with their line heights on the 4px grid, three weights, and numbers that line up.

Stableutilities.cssbase.csstokens.css

Every piece of text in the product is one of the type roles below. A role fixes the face, the size, the line height and the weight together, so a label looks like a label on every page. Body text is 14 on a 20 line; everything else steps from there on the 4px grid.

  • Space Grotesk comes from Google Fonts. It's for titles and figures only: in the audit it rendered body text 572 times, which is what the roles stop.

A twelfth class, .text-amount, isn't a role of its own: it sets a figure in medium with tabular numbers, at the size of the role around it.

Headings without a class

base.css gives the bare headings a role's look, so a heading in a component is right before anyone styles it: h1 as the page title, h2 as a section title, h3 at 18 / 28 and h4 at 16 / 24, in Space Grotesk medium. Pick the heading level for the document's outline and the class for the look; they don't have to match.

Sizes and line heights

Sizes are in rem, so they follow the reader's browser setting. Line heights are fixed lengths on the 4px grid (D2), never ratios, so text sits on the same grid as the spacing around it. --leading-tight (1) is only for single-line controls.

Weights

  • Three weights only (D6). 600 is gone, since Cera Pro has none and browsers fake it; 300 is gone, including the old count badge's.
  • Regular for reading. Medium for what's scanned for: names, labels, primary buttons, badges. Bold only for the page title.
  • A secondary button's label is regular, a step quieter than the medium of the buttons that call for action (D78).

Numbers

Figures in tables and totals use tabular numbers, every digit the same width, so a column of amounts lines up by place. base.css turns them on for every td, th and [data-numeric]; .text-amount and .text-amount-hero have them too.

  • A money field takes data-numeric: right-aligned, tabular (Field).
  • Amounts keep their cents where the source writes them, and read as money: “$12,400.00”, not “12400”.

Usage

  • Colour text with the content tokens only: primary, secondary, tertiary (Colour). Lighter than tertiary isn't readable.
  • If no role fits, the system gets a role; a page doesn't make its own.

Accessibility

  • Every text level passes 4.5:1 on the surfaces it's used on, in light and dark (D11).
  • Sizes are in rem, so text grows with the browser's font size setting.
  • On phones, fields never go below 16px: iOS zooms the page into any smaller field when it's focused (base.css).
  • Headings follow the document's outline, whatever they look like: one h1 per page, then down without skipping.

<h1 class="text-title-page">Pending jobs</h1>
<span class="text-label">Crew lead</span>
<span class="text-amount-hero">$12,400.00</span>

What's still open

  • Cera Pro is licensed and not shipped with the system: anything outside the product (this site, the published concepts) falls back to Inter.
  • Long-form reading (a proposal's text, terms, emails) has no roles of its own; the eleven roles are for the interface.
  • Whisper, the script face the audit found in the product, has no place in the system yet: kept for signatures, or retired.
  • Components set their type from tokens, not the role classes, and some step outside the roles: a card's title is 18/28 (not the card-title role's 16/24), the record's amount is 30 with a line height off the 4px grid, a phone row's name has a 22px line.
  • Nothing uses the display role. D6 lists 11 roles and Space Grotesk for titles only; utilities.css has 12 and the amounts use Space Grotesk too.
