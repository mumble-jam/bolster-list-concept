Motion

Motion says that something happened and where it went. Today the system has two durations, two curves and one rule for reduced motion; a fuller set, named by what moves, is proposed from the motion research and being tried in the motion lab.

Trying outtokens.cssbase.cssdocs/motion-research.mdconcepts/motion-lab/index.htmlUpdated 2026-10-28

Motion in Bolster is short and functional: a menu drops from its button, a dialog settles into place, a checkbox's tick grows in. It confirms that an action registered and shows where something came from. It never decorates and never makes anyone wait.

This page has two halves. Current is what system/tokens.css and the components use today (D10). Proposed is the set the motion research recommends (D124, I22): it's trying out in the motion lab and isn't in the tokens yet. Build with the current tokens.

Current: durations

Two durations cover almost everything. Fast is for things that change in place; medium is for things that arrive.

TokenValueUse
--duration-instant0ms
--duration-fast120ms
--duration-medium240ms
  • Fast, 120ms: hovers, colour changes, a tick or a radio's dot, a toggle's knob, a menu opening, a tooltip fading in.
  • Medium, 240ms: a dialog, a picker sheet, the side peek of a record, a toast arriving, the bulk bar rising.
  • Instant, 0ms: for anything that shouldn't move, written as a token so it reads as a choice.

A few components set their own timing where the two didn't fit: a button's press lands in 30ms and its release eases back in 150ms; a record panel's position and height ease over 300ms; a skeleton's shimmer loops every 1.4s.

Current: curves

TokenValueUse
--ease-standardcubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)
--ease-outcubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)
  • Ease out for things arriving: dialogs, menus, sheets, toasts, a tick growing in. They start quickly and settle.
  • Standard for things that change where they are: colour, background, a toggle's knob sliding across.

Current: reduced motion

When someone asks their system for reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce), base.css turns motion off everywhere: every transition and animation finishes at once, and smooth scrolling stops. Skeletons don't shimmer. A component adds its own rule only where something would still be out of place without its transition: a button stops lifting, and a summary card stops lifting.

One component, the bulk bar, already writes a reduced-motion fade of its own, the way the proposal below would have it. The global rule overrides it today.

Current: rules

  • Use the tokens. A transition names a duration and a curve token, never a number, unless it's one of the component timings above.
  • Name the properties. transition: opacity …, transform …, never transition: all, so nothing animates by accident.
  • Animate opacity and transform where you can: they don't make the page lay out again, so they stay smooth on a phone.
  • Use the platform. Dialogs and menus animate in and out of the top layer with @starting-style and allow-discrete; no script decides when they're visible.
.menu {
  opacity: 0;
  transform: translateY(-4px);
  transition:
    opacity var(--duration-fast) var(--ease-out),
    transform var(--duration-fast) var(--ease-out),
    overlay var(--duration-fast) allow-discrete,
    display var(--duration-fast) allow-discrete;
}
.menu:popover-open { opacity: 1; transform: none; }
@starting-style { .menu:popover-open { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(-4px); } }

Proposed: tokens by what moves

Proposed, trying out (D124). These come from the motion research, which compares what Material 3, Carbon, Fluent, Atlassian, Primer, Polaris and others agree on. They aren't in tokens.css until they're approved.

Instead of fast and medium, durations are named for what moves, and each size has a quicker exit than its entrance: leaving is quicker than arriving, so a dismissed thing never holds anyone up.

TokenValueFor
--duration-instant0msFocus rings, anything the keyboard did, movement under reduced motion
--duration-feedback100msHover, a press's release, colour and state changes
--duration-small-in150msTooltips, menus, popovers, a tick
--duration-small-out100msThe same, leaving
--duration-move200msIndicators, rows making room, reordering, expand and collapse
--duration-medium-in250msDialogs, toasts, the bulk bar
--duration-medium-out150msThe same, leaving
--duration-large-in350msBottom sheets, side panels
--duration-large-out250msThe same, leaving
--duration-emphasis700msA highlight that fades: a card just dropped, a new row
--duration-shimmer2000msOne sweep of a skeleton's loop
TokenValueFor
--ease-entercubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)Arriving: decelerates into place
--ease-exitcubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 1, 1)Leaving: accelerates away
--ease-movecubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)Moving on the page, with both ends on screen
--ease-springa linear() spring, bounce 0.15What a hand lets go of: a dropped card, a sheet springing back
--ease-linearlinearLoops, progress, a fade on its own

From today's tokens

TodayBecomes
--duration-fast 120--duration-feedback 100 and --duration-small-in 150
--duration-medium 240--duration-medium-in 250, with --duration-medium-out 150 for exits
--ease-standard--ease-move
--ease-out--ease-enter

The old names would stay as aliases while components move over, so nothing breaks on the day the tokens land.

Still open in the proposal: whether exits use the accelerating curve or the same ease-out as entrances; whether a tooltip waits 300ms (proposed) or 150ms (as the CSS tooltip does now); whether a press shrinks as well as sinks.

Proposed: how motion behaves

The research also proposes contracts for how motion behaves, whatever its timing (I22, trying out). The lab's playgrounds implement them.

  • Interruptible. A motion turned around halfway, such as a dialog closed while it opens, goes back from where it is, never from its end.
  • Focus never waits. Focus is in a dialog, a menu or a sheet the moment it opens; arrowing through tabs doesn't queue behind the indicator.
  • From where it came. Menus and popovers grow from the corner at their button, from 0.96, never from nothing; sheets from the bottom; content slides only the way someone went.
  • Waits are motion too. A tooltip waits 300ms, then the next one shows at once for 300ms after. A loader shows after 300ms and stays at least 300ms, so it never blinks. A toast stays 5s, 10s with an action, paused while pointed at or focused; errors stay.
  • By hand. A dragged card or sheet follows the pointer exactly. A sheet closes when pulled past a quarter of its height or flicked, else springs back. Escape puts a dragged card back.
  • Never animated: focus rings, and anything started from a keyboard shortcut.

Proposed: reduced motion

The research proposes less motion, not none, as Apple's platforms do: movement, scaling, tilting, blur and loops go; fades and colour changes stay, at most 150ms; and what someone moves by hand still follows their hand. A dialog would fade in without growing; a sheet would fade in without sliding.

This replaces today's switch-off in base.css if it's approved. Until then the two disagree, and only one of them can stay.

The motion lab

The motion lab has a playground for each of the system's core animations, each with the current values and a "Bolster proposed" preset that uses the tokens above. Its Copy CSS names the tokens and says when a value isn't one.

What's still open

  • The motion tokens (D124) are in the motion lab, not yet in tokens.css; the system still has two durations and two curves.
  • Reduced motion turns everything off (D10); the research proposes fades instead (I22). One of the two has to go.
  • Components set their own timings instead of the tokens: the button's 30ms press and 150ms release, the summary card's 150ms, the record panel's and the table header's 300ms (past D10's 240ms ceiling), the table's 200ms, the skeleton's 1.4s loop.
  • Two tooltip waits: the CSS-only tooltip waits 150ms (I4), tooltip.js 300ms (D52).
