Shape

Seven corner radii, eleven roles that take them, and one rule for putting rounded things inside rounded things: nested corners share a centre.

Stabletokens.csscomponents/card.csscomponents/popover.csscomponents/button.csscomponents/floating-bar.cssUpdated 2026-11-05

Corners say what kind of thing something is. Large surfaces are softly rounded, controls less so, small parts least; people and statuses are round. The radius comes with the component: nobody sets a corner by hand.

Corners by role

Components take their corner from a role (D149), so a field, a button or a menu reads the same wherever it is, and a change to one role changes every thing in it. Use the role's token; the scale below is what the roles are made of.

RoleRadiusGets it
--radius-card16Cards, the table card, summary cards, the record.
--radius-window16A workspace window (Settings, a job; D144).
--radius-sheet16A bottom sheet's top corners, on phones.
--radius-menu12Menus, popovers, the combobox's list, at every width (D150).
--radius-dialog12A centred dialog.
--radius-control8Every button, whatever its role (D33), a toast's too (D150); the segmented control (D77); toolbar controls; sidebar and rail items; calendar days; the floating bar's buttons.
--radius-toast8Toasts.
--radius-field4Inputs, selects, the combobox's field.
--radius-row4The rows inside menus, popovers and the combobox's list, 8 in from a 12 edge (D35).
--radius-tooltip4Tooltips.
--radius-checkbox2Checkboxes.
--radius-fullroundPills: badges, chips, avatars, radios, dots, sheet handles, spinners, the drop line.

A few radii are worked out from the ones around them, by the rule below: a segmented control's options are 6 (its 8 less its 2px inset), the floating bar is 14 (its 8px buttons plus 6 around them, D34), an active chip's buttons are 11 (a pixel inside its 12).

Nested corners share a centre

When something rounded sits in a rounded corner, its curve follows the outer one: both arcs have the same centre (D35). So the inner radius is the outer one less the gap between their edges, the gap the same across and down:

r = R − d, where R is the outer radius, d the gap from the outer edge (its border included) to the inner edge, and r the inner radius.

  1. The card's corner, 16 (--radius-card). Its arc is the larger dashed circle, radius 16; the dot is its centre. Drawn here larger than life.
  2. The button's corner, 8 (--radius-control), its edge 8 in from the card's outer edge. Its arc has the same centre, so the band between the two curves stays 8 wide all the way round.
  3. The gap, --inset-card: 16 − 8 = 8. The card's padding is the gap less its 1px border, 7.

Where the rule applies

  • Only in the curve. Something further in than the outer radius, the text in a card's body for one, is free. Text keeps its own inset either way: a footer's range and a record's title start where the table's cells do.
  • Pills are free. Their ends are half their height, whatever sits around them.
  • Cards are 16 so their 8px controls can sit 8 in: the table card's bar, its footer's Load more, the record's header.
  • Menus and popovers are 12 with 4px rows, 8 in (--inset-menu). On phones they become bottom sheets with their own corners.
  • The floating bar is 14 around 8px buttons, 6 in.

The insets

Two tokens hold the gaps, worked out from the radii so they change with them. The padding is the inset less the border.

These are the gaps, from the outer edge; the padding is the gap less the border.

/* rows 8 in from a menu's edge, its 1px border included */
.pop-body { padding: 0 calc(var(--inset-menu) - var(--border-width-default)); }

Checking a page

scripts/check-radii.js lists every corner on a page that breaks the rule: each rounded element sitting in a rounded parent's curve whose radius isn't the parent's less the gap. It only sees what's on screen, so open the menus, sheets, the record and the bulk bar first, and run it at 1440, 1024 and 375 wide.

await import("/scripts/check-radii.js")
__radii()

Usage

  • A radius that isn't in the table is a new decision for the system, not a page's.
  • Something new inside a card, a menu or a bar takes its corner from the rule, using the inset tokens.
