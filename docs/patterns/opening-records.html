Opening a record

A row click does one of two things, decided by the thing, not the page: it peeks at the record beside the list, or it opens the thing's own window. A row carries no buttons; its next step leads its record.

Stablecomponents/record.csscomponents/data-table.csspatterns/list-page.cssUpdated 2026-11-02

Every list says what clicking a row does, and it's one of two. Peek: the record opens beside the list, which keeps its place, for lists people triage. Open: the row goes straight to the thing's own full-screen window, for things people work in. Which one is a fact of the thing (does it have a window of its own?), so the same thing opens the same way from every list.

ForThings without a window of their own: invoices, customers, the catalog, for now.Things with a full-screen window under Edit or something like it: jobs (the job workspace), subs and vendors (the vendor's window).

The record's own headerThe expand icon opens the full record in place; its tooltip names it (“Open invoice”).The record's More leaves Open and Quick look out: you're in it already.

A quick look first is a detour on every row for something people work in, so a thing with its own window opens it (D69). The record beside the list stays a menu item away. Vendor orders and expenses are still to be settled: whether they have a window of their own is an open question in their models.

What a row click is, and isn't

  • Anywhere on the row opens it, except its checkbox. A checkbox click only ticks.
  • The row's name is a real link (D17), whichever way the row opens, so Cmd-click or a middle click opens it in a new tab, and the keyboard reaches it.
  • Open never opens a new tab. The expand icon (⤢) means it grows into its full view in the same tab; ↗ is kept for what leaves the app (D40).

No buttons on rows

A row carries no buttons. Where a page today shows a per-row button that changes with the status (Send, Pending jobs, Archive), that's the row's next step, and it leads the record: first among the record's actions, and first in the row's ··· menu after Open, never twice (D43). The record, one click away, replaces the button on the row. A destructive action can't be a next step.

The record beside the list

From 1280 wide the record is a card beside the list that sticks under the bars and scrolls on its own; the list narrows to its main columns (name, status and amount below 1440) and marks the open row with the accent edge. The bulk bar stays centred over the list, clear of the record. Below 1280 it's a side sheet over the page. Its header holds the record's name, which wraps rather than being cut short, then open and close level with its first line. See Record.

The window

A thing's own window is a full-screen dialog (the job workspace, I7). It opens with the dialog's motion; focus goes to its heading and comes back to the row's link when it closes. Escape closes it, unless a spreadsheet cell is being edited, when Escape cancels the edit first. Its close button is named after it: “Close job”. The concepts don't build the windows: opening one says so in a toast.

Keyboard and focus

  • Tab reaches each row's name link (and its checkbox, where the list has selection); Enter on the link opens the row.
  • The record takes focus on its heading. Escape, or Close, closes the sheet and returns focus to the row it came from.
  • If that row is gone (it was archived from the record), focus goes to the list's heading. Never to the page's body (I11).
  • Open and close are icon buttons with their names as tooltips (“Open job”, “Close”).

What it replaces

  • A click that selected a row and a double click that opened it, with rows the keyboard couldn't reach.
  • “Open job ↗”, which read as a new tab when it opened the job in the same one.
  • A job modal with an unlabelled close button, whose ?job= came back after moving to another page.
  • Per-row buttons that changed with the status, crowding the rows.
