Page scrolling

The page is the one scroller: it scrolls down, never a card inside it. Headers stick to the page under the bars, and a part wider than the screen scrolls sideways only: with a scrollbar at the window's foot when it sits on the page, the card's own when it's in a card.

Stablepatterns/list-page.cssconcepts/shared/shell.jsUpdated 2026-10-30

Every page scrolls the same way, the lists' way. What's longer than the screen makes the page longer; nothing inside it gets a scrollbar of its own going down. Headers that need to stay in view stick to the page, under the top bar, and slide up with it when it hides. The rule was written for a small office laptop's screen above all, where a card that scrolls inside a page that also scrolls leaves very little room to work in.

The rule

DirectionWhat happens
DownThe page scrolls, never a card, a column or a part of one. A tall part stretches to fill the screen when it's shorter than it (the calendar's Week and Day) and grows past it, making the page longer, when it isn't.
What staysHeaders stick to the page, not to a card: the table's column header, the timeline's scale and its job rows, the calendar's weekdays and all-day row, the pipeline's column heads. They sit at --page-top, under the top bar and the page's toolbar where it has one, and slide up with them when they hide.
AlongA part wider than the screen (a table, the board, the timeline's days) scrolls sideways on its own: a trackpad's swipe, a tilted wheel, Shift and the wheel, a finger, or its scrollbar: pinned to the window's foot for a part on the page itself, the card's own for a part in a card, the timeline the one exception (D141). The page itself never scrolls sideways.
Not coveredWhat floats over the page scrolls inside itself: popovers, menus, sheets, dialogs and side panels such as the record beside a list. They aren't part of the page, so the page can't carry them.

Why one scroller

  • Room. A card that scrolls inside the page has to fit a screen less the bars, the header and the toolbar. On a laptop that left the calendar's day a few hours tall. Letting the page scroll gives the work the whole height.
  • One gesture, one result. The wheel, the keys and the scrollbar always move the same thing. Nothing catches the wheel halfway down a page.
  • Native sticking. Down is the scroll people do most. Done by the browser, headers stick on the scroll itself and never trail a frame behind, as a header moved by a script does (D38, D127).

The bars as you scroll

The page title scrolls away with the page. Once you're into the content, scrolling down hides the top bar (and the page's toolbar where it sticks), and a little scroll up brings them back, sliding 300ms on the standard ease-out. They also come back at once when a menu, a sheet or a dialog opens, or when focus goes into the top bar or the toolbar, so focus is never left under a bar. What sticks under them moves with them: --page-top slides to 0 while they're away.

The behaviour is the shell's, defined once (BolsterShell.scrolling, D73). A page passes where its content starts, its toolbar if it has one, and anything else it keeps in use; the shell does the rest and sets data-chrome-hidden on the root while the bars are away.

At each width

WidthWhat hides and what sticks
1024 and upThe top bar hides on the way down; the rail and the menu rise into its place while it's away. Headers stick under the top bar and the toolbar, then at the very top. A wide part on the page (the board) or the timeline has its scrollbar at the window's foot, only while it's wider than the screen; a list's table has its card's own.
768 to 1023There's no top bar (trying out: the phone's navigation reaches up to 1023, see Navigation at each width), so what sticks sits at the very top, under the page's toolbar where it has one. The pages keep their desktop layouts, so a wide part still scrolls sideways with its scrollbar at the window's foot.
Below 768No top bar either; what sticks sits at the top, or under the selection's bar while rows are ticked (D39). The floating Create shrinks to its + as you scroll down and grows back as you scroll up (D29). Wide parts become phone layouts (cards, a list, one stage at a time) rather than scrolling sideways.

Wider than the screen

A scroller can't scroll sideways without also catching what sticks to the page, so wide parts scroll along in one of two ways, both keeping the page the only scroller down. Which one follows where the part sits (D141): a part in a card scrolls inside its card, with the card's own scrollbar at its end; a part on the page itself keeps a scrollbar at the window's foot. The timeline is the one exception: it's in a card, but moving through time is mostly scrolling sideways, so its scrollbar stays in reach.

  • The lists' tables scroll sideways inside their card, the checkbox and the name pinned on the left, a rule at their edge once you've scrolled and a fade on the right while there's more. The column header is moved down with the page by a scroll-driven animation tied to the table's place, so it stays under the bars without trailing (D38).
  • The board, on the page, and the timeline, the exception, keep a scrollbar of their own pinned to the window's foot (.page-hscroll), so a mouse with no sideways wheel always has one in reach, not at the bottom of a long page. It's drawn by hand so it shows on a Mac too, where scrollbars hide, on the canvas so nothing shows through it. The timeline's days slide by the scrollbar's value under its grid, which stays; the pipeline hides the scrollbar while a card is carried.

Keyboard and focus

  • Space, Page Up and Down, and the arrows scroll the page as they would any page. There is no second scroller for the keys to get stuck in.
  • The page's scroll padding leaves room for what sticks (scroll-padding-block-start on the root), so an element that takes focus scrolls clear of the bars and headers rather than under them.
  • Tabbing into the top bar or the page's toolbar brings them back.
  • The scrollbar at the window's foot is for the pointer only: it's hidden from screen readers and out of the tab order. From the keyboard, a wide part moves by its own controls (the timeline's Today and arrows) and by focus moving into it.
  • A redraw never moves the page under the reader: see Focus and keyboard (I13).

What it replaces

  • The calendar's Week and Day, whose hours scrolled inside the card once the day was taller than the screen.
  • The timeline's first version, which scrolled both ways inside its card (D97, replaced by D127).
  • A table header moved by a script beside the scroll, which lagged in Chrome and lost its pinned cells in Safari (D38).
  • The pipeline's own sideways scrollbar, which became the system's .page-hscroll, and the timeline's, which used the platform's and was hidden on a Mac.

Code

NameWhat it does
--page-topWhere what sticks to the page sits: the top bar's height plus the page's toolbar, sliding to 0 while the bars are hidden. Use it as the sticky element's inset-block-start.
.page-hscrollA scrollbar pinned to the window's foot, for a part wider than the screen. Its child is given the part's full width; the page keeps the two in step. hidden while the part fits.
data-chrome-hiddenSet on the root by the shell while the bars are away.
 
/* a header that sticks to the page, under the bars */
.my-scale { position: sticky; inset-block-start: var(--page-top); }

<!-- the wide part's scrollbar, at the window's foot -->
<div class="page-hscroll" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" hidden><div style="inline-size: 3200px"></div></div>

// the page says where its content starts; the shell hides and brings back the bars
BolsterShell.scrolling({ into: (top) => board.getBoundingClientRect().top < top, toolbar });
