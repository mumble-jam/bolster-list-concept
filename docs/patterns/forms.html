Forms

Forms fill themselves (D140): they start from what's there, and every value says where it came from. Underneath, wherever fields remain, a form done well: one column with labels above, errors said in words, one way to save, Save never switched off, and leaving with changes asks once.

Direction C, with this underneath. On 29 Oct 2026 direction C of the forms research was picked (D140): forms that fill themselves, from a call, an enquiry, a receipt or the job's progress, each value marked with where it came from, the record a page whose values change in place. This page is the layer underneath, and it still holds wherever fields remain: creates, settings, a value's editor and the groups of fields that belong together. C's own rules wait for its research; the three are built side by side in the Forms, three ways concept (concepts/forms).

One way to save

Every form saves the same way, so nobody has to learn which one does what: change what you need, then press Save. Nothing saves by itself, and nothing is lost without asking.

  • A create asks for what the thing needs to exist (who it's for, what it's called) and makes nothing until it's saved. People and companies are looked up first, so nobody makes a second Lakeside Drywall.
  • An edit opens the record's form, in the same place a create of its size would, and keeps the changes until Save.
  • Settings save by their section's Save, switches included, so nothing changes by brushing past it.
  • A document (an estimate, an invoice, a vendor order) is a page or the record's window, never a dialog.

Where a form lives

SizeWhere, and its SaveExample
Up to about five fieldsA dialog (Dialog), Save in its footerNew vendor, a pipeline step's question
A related recordA side sheet beside what it belongs to, Save at its footA contact on a customer, a line item's detail
A document with money, or anything longerThe page, or the record's window, Save at the window's footAn estimate, an invoice, editing a job, company settings
On phonesA full-screen sheet, its Save pinned at the footAny of the above

Layout

One column, at most the reading width (768). Labels sit above their fields: the eye goes from label to field in about a tenth of the time it takes with labels on the left. Short fields that belong together share a row, and stack on phones. Sections are fieldsets with a heading.

Widths

Every line ends at the form's edge. A field alone on its line stretches, whatever its answer: its label says what's asked. Short fields that belong together share a row, one of them stretching and the others as wide as their answers, so a ZIP doesn't run as long as the street, and the row still reads as one line: data-width 4, 8, 12, 20 or 32 characters. A row whose fields all have a width stretches its last; on phones the row stacks and each field takes the full width. Fields that share a row have no hint of their own, which would push their control out of line with the others: the section's hint says it.

The words around a field

  • A label, always, visible and above the field. A placeholder is never a label: it vanishes as you type and reads as already filled in. An icon may lead a row; it never replaces the words.
  • A hint goes between the label and the field (.field-hint), so it's read before typing: one short line saying where to find the answer or why it's asked.
  • Mark the minority, in words. Where most fields are required (a create), the others say “(optional)”; where most are optional (settings), the needed ones say “(required)”. No asterisks. A form where everything is required says nothing.

Checking

Nothing is red on a form nobody has typed in. A field is checked when it's left after a change; once it's in error, it's checked as you type and clears the moment it's right. Pressing Save checks everything. In the research's best-known study, checking on leaving a field gave 22% more successes and took 42% less time than checking only on submit.

Errors

  • Under the field, with an icon and words, never colour alone (.field-error, tied to the field by aria-describedby).
  • The words say what to do, in the label's words: “Enter company name”, “Quantity must be a number”, “ZIP isn't in the right format”. Never “Invalid”, “Please” or “This field is required”.
  • A failed save with two or more errors, or one out of sight, shows a summary at the top of the form with a link to each, and focus goes there (.form-summary). Fixing a field takes it off the list.
  • Everything typed stays, the fields that passed included.

Saving

Save lives with the form, where its kind of form keeps it, and it's always the same button: the form's primary action, named for what it saves (“Save vendor”, “Save changes”, “Save address”).

WhereSaveLeaving with changes
A dialogIn its footer, with CancelClosing asks once: Keep editing, Discard, Save
A side sheetAt its foot, with CancelClosing asks once
A page, or the record's windowIn actions that stay at the window's foot while the form is on screen, beside a note counting what isn't saved yetCancel, or leaving, asks once; a reload or a closed tab gets the browser's own prompt
SettingsOne Save per section, switches includedLeaving asks once

Save at the window's foot

On a longer form the actions stay at the window's foot, so Save is always in reach without scrolling back, and the note beside them says how much isn't saved yet. On phones it's the sheet's pinned action.

Leaving with changes

Closing or leaving a form with unsaved changes asks once, in a dialog: Keep editing (the default), Discard, Save. Nothing changed, nothing asked.

Save is never off

A disabled Save says something is wrong without saying what, and gloves, glare and a hurry make it worse. Save stays on; pressed, it shows what's missing. It's off only while saving, busy and saying “Saving…” (I12).

Inputs for the trade

  • Money: the currency outside the field, a decimal keypad, dollars and commas accepted, cents optional (data-numeric, an .input-group).
  • Quantities: the unit after the number (sq ft, LF, each).
  • Dates: typed or picked (Calendar).
  • Phone and email: type="tel" and type="email", with autocomplete, so phones bring the right keyboard and browsers fill them in.
  • Line items: a grid with keyboard movement on wide screens, summary rows with a detail sheet on phones, and a footer that adds them up against the document's total. Not in the system yet.

Accessibility

  • Every field has a label tied to it; groups are fieldsets with a legend.
  • Hints and errors are tied to their field by aria-describedby; a field in error is aria-invalid.
  • The summary is an alert that takes focus; the count of unsaved changes is announced politely as it changes.
  • Personal fields carry autocomplete (WCAG 1.3.5); what was typed earlier in a flow is offered again (3.3.7).
  • Money and legal commitments (sending, approving, paying) are checked, confirmed or reversible (3.3.4).

Every variant and state

Its specimen, live: the same markup and CSS a page uses, working (point, press, type), in this page's theme.

What's still open

  • C's rules are drafted, waiting for review (docs/forms-c-research.md, 06): records with labels beside values, values that belong together edited as one, documents in the trade's layout; marks naming the source, evidence one click away, no percentages; checks gated by the step, three severities, a summary before the step. None is in the system yet.
  • C's parts are drafts in concepts/forms: a value changed in place, the source marks, the invoice as a document with its states. The model's four properties per field (neededBy, from, editable, usedBy) and a form recipe aren't started.
  • No side sheet for a form yet: the record panel (D30) and the dialog's sheet variant are close.
  • Still open: a value saved by Enter alone or by its Save; which sources first; whether a sent invoice or estimate is changed and resent or frozen behind “Reopen for changes”; labels on settings pages; which forms work offline.
