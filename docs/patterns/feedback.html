Feedback and undo

Say what happened where it happened. A change that can be undone happens at once and offers Undo; only what can't be undone asks first, and only there is the filled red button.

Stablecomponents/toast.csscomponents/dialog.csscomponents/button.cssconcepts/shared/shell.jsUpdated 2026-11-05

Every action gets an answer, in the place it was taken. A button that's working says so on itself; a field that's wrong says so under itself; a change made on a list, a board or a calendar shows where it happened, and a toast names it with Undo. Nothing asks “Are you sure?” about something that can be taken back.

Where feedback goes

What happenedWhere it's said
An action is runningOn its button: a spinner and a progressive label (“Saving…”), the button keeping its place and width (I12). See Button.
A field is wrongOn the field: its edge and a message under it saying what to do (D7). See Field.
A change was madeIn place (the task struck through, the card in its new column, the bar on its new day), and a toast naming it, with Undo.
Something failedA toast that stays until dismissed and says what to do next; a list that can't load shows the error banner in its place, with Try again (see States).
Something can't be undoneA confirm dialog, before (below).

Toasts

A toast is short feedback on an action the person just took. It never takes focus and never stands in for an answer that belongs on the screen itself.
TriggerThe result of an action the person just took. Never news from elsewhere.
MotionRises 8px and fades in over 240ms. It leaves the way it came, while its place closes up, so the toasts above it come down rather than drop. With reduced motion it only fades.
TimingSuccess and info leave after 5 seconds, paused while pointed at or focused. Errors stay until dismissed.
FocusNever moves focus. Undo and Dismiss are reachable by Tab.
PlacementBottom right, stacked; on phones full width at the bottom. Over a modal (the job workspace, a dialog), toasts show inside it, so they stay above it.
SemanticsOne aria-live="polite" region per page; an error is role="alert".

Undo sits on the inverse surface, which flips between the themes, so it has an action colour of its own, --color-action-brand-on-inverse: the brand yellow on the dark toast, the yellow's darkest shade on the light one, both well past AA at rest and hovered (D125). See Toast.

The words

  • Say what happened to what, in the past tense: “Garage drywall moved to Booked”, “Task completed”, “Invoice 1042 sent”. Name the new value when it moved: the day, the stage.
  • An error says what to do next, in plain words: “That job is private. Ask its owner to share it.”, not “That is a private object.”
  • No exclamation marks, no “Successfully”.

Confirm or undo

The rule: Undo for what can be undone, a confirm dialog for what can't, and the filled danger button only there.

  • Can be undone: completing a task, moving a card or a bar, sending an estimate from the pipeline, deleting a calendar event. It happens at once, shows in place, and the toast offers Undo. Asking first would slow every one of them down to protect against a mistake Undo already fixes.
  • Can't be undone: deleting for good, and anything else the system can't put back. A dialog asks first, names exactly what will happen, and its final button is the filled danger (data-variant="danger"), labelled with the action and its object.
  • Needs something to go ahead: a step that asks for a value (a contract date, a reason it was lost) is a short dialog of its own, not a confirmation. Its Cancel or Escape leaves everything as it was.

In a confirmation, focus starts on the safe choice (“Keep jobs”), Escape and the close button cancel, and a click on the backdrop does nothing, since something could be lost (I1). Which of Bolster's actions fall on each side (archive, void an invoice, cancel a payment) isn't listed yet; the bulk bar's Archive, which can be undone, confirms today.

What stays put

Feedback never rearranges the page around the person. Anything that updates while they act elsewhere (filters, search, sort, selection, paging) changes its values, never its layout (I13):
StatesFull, few, none, loading and error fill the same slots at the same size. A state is said by what a slot holds (“2 of 4”, “None decided yet”, an empty bar), never by adding or removing lines.
SizesCome from the data, not the screen: tabular figures, and every column but the first as wide as the widest thing it can ever show. Sorting, loading more or a new status can't resize or clip anything.
Optional partsLeave a placeholder: a bar becomes an empty track, a figure a word.
What comes and goesAppears where the person acted and pushes only what's below it, or floats over the page (the bulk bar, toasts, the side panel).
A done taskStays where it was, struck through, until the list is next drawn, so the row under the pointer doesn't change (I17).

Accessibility

  • One polite live region per page reads toasts as they come; errors interrupt (role="alert").
  • A toast never takes focus, so it never pulls someone out of what they're doing; its Undo is a real button.
  • A busy button keeps aria-busy="true" and stays enabled for screen readers, so they don't lose their place.
  • A confirmation is labelled by its heading and described by its sentence (aria-describedby).

What it replaces

  • An error toast that stayed across page changes, with copy that didn't say what to do.
  • Undo in brand yellow on a light toast in dark theme, at 1.02:1 (D125).
  • Lists whose columns resized as they were sorted, and a card that swapped its bar for a line of text as filters changed.
