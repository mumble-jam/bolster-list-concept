Drag and drop

Moving tasks, cards, bars and appointments by hand: where it will land before you let go, what a drop does or asks, Escape to put it back, Undo after, and a keyboard way to make every move.

Draftcomponents/drop-line.cssconcepts/todo/todo.jsconcepts/pipeline/pipeline.cssconcepts/calendar/calendar.jsconcepts/timeline/timeline.jsUpdated 2026-11-05

Draft. Each page that drags was built on its own: Tasks, the calendar, the pipeline and the timeline. What they share is written here; the drop line is in the system, the pipeline's slot is waiting for the product designer's approval, and a single rule for which a new page uses is still open.

Some things are moved by hand: a task to another day, a card to the next stage, a job's bars along the weeks. A drag always shows where it will land before it's let go, says when a drop would ask for something or isn't allowed, and can be cancelled with Escape or undone after. Nothing can only be moved by dragging: every move has a keyboard way.

Where it will land

Two markers show the landing place, by what's being moved:

MarkerUsed whereWhat it is
The drop lineTasks, where the order is yoursA line in the seam between two rows, from the check to the row's end, a dot at its start, in the accent blue (.drop-line). Never a row's own edge: its rounded corners read as picking that row (D104).
The slotThe pipeline, where the order is the date'sA space the card's size opens where the drop puts it, the cards easing apart for it (160ms), dashed in the accent blue on the card's ground (D121). It moves only when the landing place changes, not with every move of the pointer.

Where the order isn't yours, the marker shows where the drop puts it, not where the pointer is: on the pipeline, the group the step puts it in, at its date's place, under a folded group's head. Where a drop isn't allowed, there's no marker.

While dragging

  • The thing follows the pointer, lifted under it. Its own place stays as a dashed outline with its contents faded, so from and to read together.
  • A dark tag follows the pointer and says what letting go would do: the new day and time and person on the calendar, the dates and working days on the timeline, the step on the pipeline (“Create its job, in Pending”, “Mark as booked: contract and start”), or why it can't (“One stage at a time: Booked first”).
  • Targets: on the pipeline the stages it can go to are dashed, the one under the pointer lit, the rest faded. On the calendar the days it would cover light up.
  • Near an edge of the board, the chart or the page, it scrolls.

Letting go

  • A drop that's complete saves at once, with a toast naming what changed (the new day, the new stage) and Undo. On the timeline, what waits on a stage moves by as much when it went later, and the toast names it.
  • A drop that needs something opens the step's short dialog (a contract date, a reason it was lost). Its Cancel or Escape leaves everything as it was.
  • A refused drop puts the thing back: a stage the card can't reach; on the timeline, a link to another job's stage or one that would make a circle, refused with a toast.
  • On the pipeline, the card in its new place keeps focus and shows a brief accent ring.

By page

PageWhat movesRules
TasksA row, by its handle (shown on hover)Within a group it reorders. Grouped by when, a drop into another day's group gives the task that day; Overdue and Completed take no drops; grouped by job, a task stays in its job.
CalendarA bar, an appointment, a job from the Unscheduled tray; a bar's endA bar keeps its length and the day you grabbed it by; an appointment snaps to 15 minutes in Week and Day; in Crew, a drop on another row gives it to that person. A bar's end stretches it; it can't end before it starts. Busy time from other calendars doesn't move.
PipelineA cardOne stage either way. A person goes to Pending only by having its job created; a job to Getting paid only by having its invoice created. Invoices don't move by dragging. A lost card goes back only onto the stage it left.
TimelineA stage's bar, its end, a job's or phase's bracket; a linkA day at a time. A bracket moves all of it. Dragging from the circle at a bar's end onto another stage of the same job makes that stage wait for it.

Touch and phones

  • Tasks: on phones, where there's no handle, a long press (450ms, a tick of vibration where there is one) lifts the row; moving sooner is a scroll, and the press doesn't open the task.
  • The calendar: touch scrolls, as always; on phones things move from their summary.
  • The pipeline and the timeline: no dragging on phones; Move to, or Starts, in the summary sheet.
  • With a mouse or a pen, everything above.

The keyboard's way

PageKeys
TasksFocus the row's handle, then Alt and ↑ or ↓; the new place is read out. Past a group's edge, the task takes that group's day.
CalendarOpen the summary (Enter), set Starts for a bar, or Day and Starts for an appointment, then Move.
PipelineOpen the summary, pick Move to (only the stages the card can go to), then Move: the same dialogs.
TimelineOpen the stage's summary, set Starts, then Move.

Every keyboard move gives the same toast and Undo as a drag.

Cancelling and undoing

  • Escape while dragging puts it back where it was, with nothing saved.
  • Undo on the toast takes the move back.
  • Motion that's turned around goes back from where it is, never from its end (I22, proposed). A dragged thing follows the pointer one to one; with reduced motion, what a hand moves still moves, and the cards part at once.

Code

The drop line is drawn by the page's script inside the box it's drawn over (positioned absolutely: its top, start and width), only while dragging.

<div class="group" style="position: relative">
  …rows…
  <div class="drop-line" aria-hidden="true" style="inset-block-start: 132px; inset-inline-start: 12px; inline-size: calc(100% - 12px)"></div>
</div>
