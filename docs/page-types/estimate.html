Page types

Estimate

A job's estimate as a list page in the job's window: a tree of assemblies, cost items, selections and fees in the table, what's left as the cards, the job's totals at its foot, and one record for every kind of line, its form opening a step at a time.

Draftconcepts/estimate/index.htmlconcepts/estimate/estimate.cssconcepts/estimate/estimate.jsconcepts/estimate/data.jsUpdated 2026-11-04

In Bolster an estimate is built inside the job's window: the full-screen window a job opens into, with its own navigation. Today it edits one line in two editors that disagree (a side panel in List, a dialog in Sheet and Cards: other labels, other order, even other values), and the list has no sort, filter, grouping or totals by trade. This concept makes the estimate a list page, stretched to hold a tree, and gives every kind of line one record whose form opens a step at a time.

Round 2 (29 Oct), built from Bolster's live estimate and the estimation prototype (docs/research/estimate/). Everything new here is a draft, waiting for review; the record uses the forms' C rules (D140), which are drafts too.

Try it: the estimate concept, Marco Ruiz's “Kitchen and roof refresh”, the job the forms concept creates from his voicemail, now being priced. In the repo, Open job on the jobs list opens it too.

Who it's for

The estimator pricing a job: taking measurements, setting quantities, making the choices that are theirs, checking margins, and sending it. The customer makes the other choices on the proposal.

What a line can be

Bolster's kinds, no more. Labor, materials and subcontracted aren't kinds: they're three parts of a line's cost, and any line can have any of them.

KindWhat it isIts record's sections
AssemblyHolds other lines, to any depth. Its quantity multiplies everything in it; its cost and price add up its lines.What's in it (the split, its lines), Quantity, Pricing (one margin for all of it), Measurements here, For the customer, Production
Cost itemA quantity, typed or worked out from the job's measurements, times a cost per unit (labor, materials, subcontracted), with a margin. Bought by the piece, its materials round up to whole pieces after waste.Quantity, Pricing, For the customer, Production, Catalog
Customer's choiceA selection the customer makes on the proposal, starting from its current option. Options can vary along traits (a style and a finish).Options first, then Quantity, Pricing, For the customer (who chooses, whether they can change it), Production
Your choiceA selection the estimator makes, hidden from the proposal. Nothing is priced until it's picked.The same, its options saying what's picked
FeeA rate of the project's cost, price or labor, following it as it changes.The fee (based on, rate), Pricing, For the customer, Production

The tree in the table

  • The name's slot holds the line's photo (D55, the catalog's thumbnail): materials and the selections' current options have one; where there's none the placeholder shows the line's kind (an assembly's dark). It centres on the name and the line under it; on phones it's a column down the whole item, as the list's photos are.
  • An assembly is a row, with its own totals and a chevron; what's in it steps in 20 a level. Its margin shows as a range when its lines differ (“0%–30%”).
  • A line says what it needs, quietly. No quantity: “Set qty” in the link colour where its quantity goes (its tip says why: no number, or a measurement not taken). Your choice still open: “Choose an option” under its name, and its price $0.00. An optional line left out: “Not included” where its price goes. Nothing is red until Send.
  • Marks stay small: a calculator before a quantity worked out from measurements, AutoCost where the price follows the supplier, Optional. Your choices say so in the line under the name (“Choose an option”, “Picked: …”), the customer's say “Current: …”.
  • An Add row closes every open assembly, and the estimate.

The record: a form a step at a time

  1. The head: its photo with its credit, when it has one; what kind of line it is and where it sits (its assemblies, as links), its name, four facts (quantity, cost, margin, price), and the one thing it still needs with its action (Set quantity, Choose, Add measurement, Add cost).
  2. Sections by the job they serve, each showing what's set. A value reads beside its label and turns into its field where it is (C-edit): Enter or leaving it saves, Escape puts it back. An empty value says what it's for (C-empty), or, when it's rarely set, waits at the section's foot as a chip naming it (“+ Tax”, the system's unset chip): the chip opens that one value's field, Escape puts the chip back.
  3. Values that belong together edit as one small form under their row (C-together): labor, materials and subcontracted with their switches; how it's bought (by the piece, its size, its price, the waste); one margin for a whole assembly.
  4. The deep end is a step further: a quantity can be typed or worked out from measurements, its formula with the job's measurements as chips to insert and the result as you type; a selection's traits; a fee's base.

Behaviour

WhatHow
SavingEvery change saves as it's made. The bar says Saving…, then Saved; its history lists the changes with Undo on the latest. Deleting, moving and bulk changes also offer Undo in a toast. Send is only Send.
SendingSend opens the summary before the step (C-gate): what to fix (quantities and your choices: those lines would price at $0), what to check (lines with no cost), and what the customer decides (their choices, the optional lines), each a link to its line. With nothing to fix, it sends; after that, changes wait for Resend.
AddingThe Add row becomes a field: the catalog's matches show as you type; Enter creates a cost item with that name and keeps the field for the next; arrows walk the suggestions; More adds an assembly, a selection or a fee. A line from the catalog opens on its quantity.
MeasurementsThe job's measurements open in the record's place, by assembly, each saying what it's used in. Taking one prices every line that waits for it.
NarrowingA card, a filter or a search keeps the matching lines with their assemblies around them, unfolded, and says “3 of 31 lines”; search marks the words it found.
SelectingTicking an assembly ticks what's in it; the bar's actions show only when the ticked lines qualify. Combine gathers siblings into a new assembly and names it.
LoadingOpening the estimate and a line wait as D142 says (Concept controls' Network): nothing under 300 ms, then skeletons in the shape of the rows.

Open questions

  • A booked job's estimate: changed in place (each change a change order), or locked behind “Start a change order”?
  • Margin or markup as the number people set? Bolster stores margin and shows both; this shows margin with the markup beside it.
  • Sheet and Cards: still needed once the list groups, filters and edits in its record?
  • AutoCost: what a price “kept up to date” means once the customer has seen the estimate.
  • Reordering by drag, and the catalog's full browser, aren't built.

What's still open

  • Not in the visual and accessibility tests yet (axe was run by hand: clean on nine states, light and dark, 375 to 1440).
  • The C rules it uses for the record (read, edit in place, small forms) are still drafts, here and in the forms concept; they move into system/ together once approved.
  • Sheet and Cards, reordering by drag, the catalog's full browser and the proposal (the customer's side) aren't built.
  • Links on raised surfaces in dark failed AA across the system (found here): fixed at the token, 29 Oct.
