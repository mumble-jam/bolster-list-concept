BolsterDesign system

Components

Badge

A small, static label: a job's status from its own token set, a feedback tone for anything else, or a count. The word is the cue; colour backs it up.

Stablecomponents/badge.cssUpdated 2026-10-15

When to use

  • Use a status badge for a job's status: lead, draft, pending, booked, in progress, active or outstanding. It reads its colour from data-status, so the same status looks the same on every page.
  • Use a tone badge for any other short state with a feeling to it: paid, due soon, overdue, new, not connected.
  • Use a count on a nav item or a tab, for how many things wait there.
  • Use a chip for a filter people can set and remove (Chip), and a segmented control or summary cards for a filter that toggles. A badge is never pressed.

Anatomy

  1. Mark (optional, job list only). The status drawn as a shape, before the word. See Status marks.
  2. Label. One or two words, 12px medium, sentence case.
  3. Body. A 20px pill on a tint of its tone, with a hairline of its own text colour inside the edge, so it holds its shape on a tint of the same hue.

Job status

Job status has a token set of its own (D12): a surface, a text colour and a dot colour for each, every pair at 4.5:1 or more in light and dark. Booked is Bolster green, in progress blue.

A status badge carries no dot (D61). A dot isn't an icon, and icons show only where a list asks for them. Other lists' stages (a vendor order's, an invoice's) are a plain tinted badge in their colour (D49).

Status marks

On the job list a status also wears a shape, so the shape says the status and colour only backs it up. A job fills a quarter at a time as it moves on; a slash is the client saying no. The marks are the job list's own (D49): every other list shows a stage by its colour alone.

Markdata-markMeans
emptyNot started: a lead
dashedNot sent yet
quarterWith the client
halfSigned
threeWork underway
fullClosed, paid
slashDeclined, lost
  • Once a shape carries meaning it's a graphic that must reach 3:1, so draft, pending and in progress take darker mark colours (--color-status-*-mark) than their dots.
  • The marks are drawn on a 12px grid (a 1.5px ring), crisp at 12px in a badge and at 14px on a summary card.
  • A mark can stand alone beside a label: <span class="mark" data-mark="half">, its colour set in --_dot. Summary cards do this.

Feedback tones

For anything that isn't a job status. Subtle is the default; solid is for the rare badge that must stand out on a busy surface. Square is for tags in the app's chrome and settings (an admin, a beta).

Count

A number on the accent colour, at least as wide as it is tall. It sits beside the label of what it counts: a nav item, a tab.

Usage

Accessibility

  • The word carries the meaning; colour is the second cue, and the mark a third on the job list. Never a badge with colour alone.
  • Every status and tone pair passes AA (4.5:1) for its text in light and dark; marks reach 3:1 as graphics (npm run contrast).
  • Marks are drawn by CSS and add nothing for a screen reader, which reads the word.
  • A badge is a <span>: it isn't focusable and does nothing when clicked.
