Record panel

One row's details without leaving the list: beside it on wide screens, a sheet from the side on narrower ones. Its next step comes first, then what happened, then everything it links to.

Stablecomponents/record.cssUpdated 2026-10-22

The record panel shows one row of a list in full while the list stays where it was. It reads top to bottom in the order people need it: where the thing stands and what it's worth, how it got there and what comes next, what to do now, what it's linked to, its details, then its document. The pattern around it, which rows open it and which open a window instead, is in Opening records.

When to use

  • Use the record panel on lists people triage: a quick look at a row, an action, and on to the next row (a peek, D30). Invoices, customers and the catalog open it from the row.
  • Use the thing's own window where people work on it. A thing that has one (a job's workspace, a vendor's window) opens it from the row; the record panel is then Quick look, second in the row's ··· menu after Open (D69). See Dialog, workspace.
  • Use a popover when only an action is needed: the row's ··· menu (Popover).

Where it sits

  • From 1280 wide, beside the list: a card that sticks under the bars as the page scrolls, as tall as the window allows. The list narrows and keeps its name, status and amount columns; the open row is marked.
  • Below 1280, a sheet from the right, 26rem wide over a scrim (dialog.peek), the whole height of the screen. It slides in 24px as it fades.
  • Below 768, the sheet is the full width of the screen.
  • Crossing 1280 with a record open moves it between the side and the sheet. Like everything that floats over the page, it scrolls inside itself, down only: a value too wide for it ends in an ellipsis or wraps (Page scrolling).

Anatomy

PartClassWhat it holds
Header.detail-headThe record's name as its heading, wrapping however long, then Open and Close in the card's corner, level with the name's first line.
Hero.detail-heroA photo if the thing has one, the status badge, the amount (.detail-amount, cents raised and half size), a line under it (.detail-sub: an address, a unit price), the activity and the actions.
Related.detail-relatedA link to every record the thing connects to, its kind on the left.
Fields.detail-fieldsThe rest, as a definition list: label on the left, value on the right, one line each.
Document.detail-docThe thing's document, if it has one: its name, a link to view it, the file to download (.detail-file).

Header: open and close

Open is an icon button with the expand icon, right before Close. Its tooltip and name say what opens (“Open job”, “Open invoice”, “Open customer”): the record grows into its full view in the same tab. The row's name in the list stays a real link, so a new tab is a Cmd-click away. The ↗ arrow is kept for links that leave the app (D40).

Actions: the next step first

Rows carry no buttons. A row's next step, the action that changes with its status (Send, Invoice this project, Start the work), leads its record instead, and leads the row's ··· menu after Open (D43). The record shows two actions and More on one line. When both don't fit, the second moves into More. More lists whatever isn't on the card and only shows when it has something in it. A record with nothing to do in its state (paid, cancelled) has no action row at all. Actions are secondary buttons, flat on the card.

Related records

Each record the thing connects to, as a link with its name and avatar: a vendor order's vendor and job, and the job's client through it; a job's client; an invoice's client and job. They don't repeat as fields, and actions that only go to one of them (View vendor, View job) leave the menus. The other way round too: what points at the thing, as a count that links to the list (a vendor's 4 vendor orders, 13 expenses), or “None yet” in plain text (D57). Links stay in the app, so they carry no ↗.

A photo

A row with a picture (a thumbnail in the list) shows it large at the top of its record: the card's width at 3:2, with the credit its licence asks for under it. A broken link takes the whole figure away (D55).

Activity

The record's story: what happened to the thing, oldest first, and what comes next. A line runs through the events, solid through what happened, dashed to the next step. Job events wear the status mark they moved the job to, in its colour, so the activity reads like the badge's history. Other events are small dots: the accent by default, green for money in, red for a miss. The next step is a hollow dashed ring, its words in the secondary ink.

  • Each event is what happened (.activity-what) over when and who (.activity-meta): “Sent to Ingrid”, “Oct 3”.
  • A long history folds: the first event, “+ n more events” on a dotted line (.activity-more), then the last two and the next step. The button unfolds them and focus goes to the first that appeared.
  • The line stops the same distance short of every dot, whatever its size or shape: each dot sits on a disc of the card's colour 3px wider than itself.
  • It's called Activity because Bolster's Timeline is its schedule page (Jobs → Timeline), not a record's history (D74).

Usage

Content

  • Heading: the name people know the thing by, as the list shows it (“Ingrid Ferreira · Blown-in insulation”); codes go on the line under the amount.
  • An amount that isn't set yet says so in words, quieter and smaller (“No price yet”).
  • Field labels are the list's column names. Dates read month first: “Oct 2, 2026”.
  • Teammates (Owner, Created by) stay fields; other records are Related.

Accessibility

  • The panel is a section labelled by its heading. On opening, focus moves to the heading (tabindex="-1"); it shows a focus ring only when the record was opened from the keyboard (data-quiet-focus marks a click).
  • Open's accessible name and tooltip say what opens (“Open job”); Close is named “Close”.
  • Escape closes the record, unless a menu from it is open, which closes first. Focus goes back to the row's link it came from (I6, I11).
  • The activity is an ordered list named “Activity”; its dots and lines are drawing only, the words carry the story.
