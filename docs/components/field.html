Components

Field

One frame for text inputs, text areas, native selects, money and numbers: a label above, the control, a helper below that turns into the error. Phones, emails, postal codes and numbers are tidied when the field is left, by the company's country.

Stablecomponents/field.csscomponents/field.jsUpdated 2026-11-02

When to use

  • Use a field for anything people type: a name, an address, a price, a note to the customer.
  • Use a native select for a short list of fixed choices, a handful that fit on screen. A long list people need to search (clients, items, the crew) is a combobox, and an address is the address field.
  • Use a checkbox, radio or toggle for yes or no and for two to five visible options (Checkbox, radio, toggle).
  • Use the search field to narrow a list in a toolbar; it's bare, not boxed (Search field).

Anatomy

  1. Label. Always visible, above the control, in sentence case. It names the value, not the action: “Company name”, not “Enter company name”.
  2. Optional marker (when it applies). “(optional)” in a quieter weight after the label, on the fields people can leave empty.
  3. Control. The input, text area or select, with a border that clears 3:1 against the page so it can be found.
  4. Placeholder (optional). An example of the value, in the tertiary colour. It disappears as people type, so it never carries the label or a rule.
  5. Helper. One short line on what the value is for or what shape it takes. On an error it turns red and says how to fix it.

Kinds

Every kind shares the same frame, height and border, so a form of mixed controls lines up.

  • Text input. <input class="input"> with the right type (email, tel, url), so phones bring up the right keyboard.
  • Native select. The select inside .select, which draws the chevron. The select itself stays the browser's, so its list, keyboard and phone picker are the platform's.
  • Money and quantities. data-numeric aligns the figures to the end in tabular numerals, so they read down a column. A currency sign or a unit goes in an adornment beside the value, never typed into it. See Numbers and units.
  • Text area. At least two rows tall, and people can drag it taller.

Formats

Phones, emails and postal codes are typed any way and tidied when the field is left, never while it's typed: the caret never jumps and a paste is never mangled (D136). Only a right value is tidied; a wrong one stays as typed, and the form says how to fix it, in words. The country is the company's, or an address's own (data-country).

Numbers and units

Numbers are typed, with no − and + buttons; ↑ and ↓ step them by their step, Shift by ten (D137). The unit sits after the number, in the words people say. When the field is left, the number is grouped and rounded to its unit's places; money always shows its cents. Empty stays empty, never 0.

  • Grouping follows the country: 1,250.5 in the US, 1.250,5 in Germany. A group mark without three digits after it is a mistake: 12,5 in the US asks for a number rather than becoming 125.
  • Limits (data-min, data-max) say so in the label's words: “Markup must be 100 or less”.
  • Bolster writes ft2 in its pricebook; the system writes sq ft.

Sizes

Fields share the control scale with buttons and segmented controls (D5): a field and the button beside it are the same height. Set the size on .field; the control, its padding and an adornment group follow.

  • Small in dense places: a table's cells, the From and To of the date range picker.
  • Medium by default, for forms and dialogs.
  • Large for touch-first forms, with 16px text.

A form uses one size throughout, its selects included. The audited product mixed 48px fields with 40px selects on the same form.

States

Hover and focus are held in place here. Focus doesn't use the outer ring other controls draw: the border turns blue and gains a 1px inner ring, 2px in all (D8).

Every state, held in place, is in the live specimen at the end of this page.

An adornment group (.input-group) takes its control's states: hover, focus, error, disabled and read only.

  • Error. aria-invalid="true" on the control turns its border red, and the helper below turns red with it. The red stays while focused.
  • Disabled. A grey fill, no border, muted text. It can't be focused or sent with the form, so use it only for what can't apply here, and say why nearby.
  • Read only. A tinted fill, no border, the secondary text colour. It can be focused, selected and copied: use it for values the system sets, like a company id.

Usage

  • One size per form, selects included.
  • Never override a field's height, border or corners. If a kind is missing, it's added to the system.

Content

  • Labels are nouns in sentence case: “Billing address”, “Unit price”. No colon, no “Enter…”.
  • Placeholders are examples in the value's own shape: “name@company.com”, “0.00”.
  • Helpers are one line: what the value is used for (“Shown on proposals”) or what it needs.
  • Errors say how to fix it: “Enter a full email address, like name@company.com”, not “Invalid email”.

Accessibility

  • Every control has a <label for>. Where a label can't show (a search in a toolbar), the control takes an aria-label.
  • The helper is tied to the control with aria-describedby, so it's read with the field, and so is the error once the helper says it.
  • aria-invalid="true" marks the error for screen readers as well as drawing it. Colour is never the only sign: the helper's words change too.
  • The border is 4.0:1 on white, past the 3:1 a control's edge needs (the audited border was 2.9:1).
  • inputmode="decimal" on money and quantities brings up a number keyboard on phones without the quirks of type="number".
  • Native selects keep the platform's keyboard and phone pickers. Don't rebuild one.

What's still open

  • Phones outside the US and Canada are kept as written, not grouped by each country's own rules (that takes a library such as libphonenumber).
  • No hint for a likely typo in an email's domain (gmial.com).
  • Numbers are typed only (D137): − and + buttons wait for a case. The first candidate: the pipeline's Schedule dialog, Working days (1 to 60).
  • Do estimators type formulas (12*8) into quantities today? A quantity takes a number only.
