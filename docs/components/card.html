Components

Card

The surface things sit on: an optional header, a body, 16px corners and a quiet raised body. A linked card opens what it's about from anywhere on it.

Stablecomponents/card.cssUpdated 2026-11-02

When to use

  • Use a card to group what belongs together on the page: a list (the table card), a to-do group, a block of settings.
  • Use a linked card when the whole card stands for one thing that opens: a job, a settings page.
  • Use summary cards for the figures above a list. They share the card's body, but they're buttons.

Anatomy

  1. Surface. 16px corners, a white line along the top, a darker bottom edge and two soft shadows tinted with the page: the card's body, all tokens (below).
  2. Title (optional). An h2 or h3 in the header, 18px; it takes the room it needs and the rest of the header follows it.
  3. Header actions (optional). A ghost button or two, at the header's end.
  4. Content. 20px in from the sides, 20px above the card's foot; or flush, for content that runs to the card's edges.

The card's body

Cards read as surfaces, so the buttons on them stay the things you press. Light falls from above: a white line along the top (it carries the edge in dark, where shadows hardly show), a darker bottom edge, a faint sheen and shade, a tight contact shadow that seats the card and a soft ambient one that lifts it, both tinted with the warm page. A card doesn't move; only summary cards, being buttons, lift and press.

Every value is a token, the same in every product and prototype: copy the tokens, never the computed shadow. --shadow-card at rest, with -hover, -pressed and -selected for cards you press (summary cards); each is the same six layers (the selected ring, the top line, the bottom lip, a press, the contact and the ambient shadow), so one eases into the next. The edge is --color-line-card with --color-line-card-edge along the bottom, the sheen and shade --gradient-card, the corners --radius-card (16). Dark sets the shadows again, 2.5 times as strong, the top line fainter (D148).

Anything on a card that would lift on the page lies flat on it instead: a secondary button drops its own body, since the surface is raised already (Button).

data-elevation="flat" sets one card in the page instead: its hairline edge, no body. data-cards="flat" on the root does it for every card (the concept's switch, to compare).

Linked card

Put an <a class="card-link"> around the title. The link stretches over the whole card, so a click anywhere opens it, and the card wears the one focus ring when the link is focused. It's a real link: it can open in a new tab, and a screen reader lists it by its title.

The stretched link lies over everything else on the card, so a control inside a linked card can't be reached by a click. Keep linked cards to a title and text.

Nested corners

Something rounded inside a card keeps the card's curve: its radius is the card's 16 less the gap between them (D35). Controls with 8px corners sit 8 in (--inset-card), as in the table card's toolbar, its Load more and an error banner. Text keeps its own inset.

Filter cards

A first draft, kept for pages that aren't lists yet: a radio group of cards that filter what's under them, one tab stop with arrow keys between. On list pages summary cards replace them.

Usage

Accessibility

  • Use a <section> with a heading, so the card has its place in the page's outline; the heading level follows the page's.
  • A linked card is one link, named by its title. Its focus ring is drawn around the card, not the title.
  • Filter cards are native radios: one tab stop, arrow keys move between them, the group named by aria-label.
