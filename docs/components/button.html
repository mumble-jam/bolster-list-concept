Button

One button, named by its role: primary for the main action, brand for the step that moves a job forward, secondary for the rest, ghost in toolbars and rows, danger for what can't be undone.

Stablecomponents/button.cssUpdated 2026-10-25

When to use

  • Use a button for an action: it does something here (saves, sends, opens a menu or a dialog).
  • Use a link to go somewhere. A row or a card that opens a record is a link stretched over it, never a button (see Data table and Card).
  • Use a segmented control to switch between views or modes of the same thing (Segmented control).
  • Use a menu when there are more than three actions of equal weight: one button opens them (Menu).

Anatomy

  1. Body. An edge, a lighter top line and a small shadow. It lifts 1px when pointed at and sinks at once when pressed. Secondary drops its body on anything already raised.
  2. Icon (optional). Before the label, sized by the button. Leave it out unless it helps people find the button faster.
  3. Label. A verb and what it acts on, in sentence case: “Schedule visit”, not “Schedule” or “VISIT”.

Roles

A button is named by what it's for, never by its colour. Blue isn't a button colour: blue means a link, focus or selection.

RoleLooks likeUse it for
primaryThe main action of a view. One per view. Black with highlighter text in light; yellow with black text in dark.
brandThe single step that moves a job forward: sending a proposal, approving, getting paid. Rare on purpose; it's Bolster's yellow.
secondaryEverything else. Lifted on the page, flat on a card, a dialog, a menu or a bar: the surface decides, nobody picks.
ghostToolbars, rows and anywhere a body would be noise. Icon-only buttons are usually ghost.
ghost · dangerA destructive action among others: red text, red fill when pointed at.
dangerOnly the final button of a confirmation, for what can't be undone.

Secondary on the page and on a card

On the page a secondary button has its body and lifts. On anything already raised it lies flat, since the surface is lifted already. The surface says once that it's raised (--surface-raised in base.css); the button reads it.

Sizes

Heights come from the control scale shared with fields and segmented controls, so things in a row line up. Medium is the default.

  • Small in toolbars, tables and dense headers. On touch screens it gets a 44px hit area without drawing bigger.
  • Medium for forms, dialogs and page headers.
  • Large for a form's main action on a phone.
  • Extra large only in the field app, for the one action a crew lead taps with gloves on.

States

Hover, press and focus are shown here held in place. Focus is the system's one ring (2px, 2px off the edge, in the focus blue).

Every state, held in place, is in the live specimen at the end of this page.

Busy

While an action runs, the button keeps its place and its width, shows a spinner and says what it's doing (“Saving…”). It ignores further clicks. When the action ends, the button goes back, and the result is said where it happened (a toast, or the error on the field). See Focus and keyboard (I12).

With icons

  • An icon-only button is square, has an aria-label, and a tooltip with the same words (data-tip, D52).
  • A button that opens a picker reads back what's picked in a .button-value (“Last 90 days”), its icon quieter. Its own name stays a command where nothing's picked (“Add filter”).
  • Icons side by side match in ink and line, not in box size: one drawn smaller goes a weight up (D41).

Usage

  • Show an action only when it applies. If a button must be disabled, say why nearby (a tooltip or the helper text).
  • Never override a button's height, corners or colours. If a variant is missing, it's added to the system.
  • A row's next step isn't a button on the row: it leads the row's record (D43).

Accessibility

  • A <button> for actions, an <a class="button"> for navigation; both take every role and size.
  • Enter and Space activate; the focus ring shows on keyboard focus only.
  • Icon-only buttons are named by aria-label and the tooltip says the same.
  • aria-busy="true" while busy; keep the label, so a screen reader hears “Saving…”.
  • A button that opens something says so: aria-haspopup="menu" or "dialog", and aria-expanded while it's open.
  • Every role passes AA for its label in light and dark (npm run contrast).

Its specimen, live: the same markup and CSS a page uses, working (point, press, type), in this page's theme.

What's still open

  • Primary's hover is still open: invert (the prototype) or darken (the live app), D4.
  • No split button (a main action with a menu of its siblings: Save and Save as), which Bolster uses on records.
  • No button group for a run of related buttons that aren't a segmented choice.
