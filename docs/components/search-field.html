Search field

Bare in a toolbar, with a hover fill and the focus ring on the whole field; on the dark top bar, a lit well with ⌘K as keycaps; on a page's own toolbar, trying out, a button that grows into its field.

Stablecomponents/toolbar.csscomponents/shell.cssUpdated 2026-11-02

When to use

  • Use the toolbar's search (.list-search) to narrow what a page already shows: a list's rows, a board's cards, the to-do list. It searches this page only.
  • The top bar's search finds anything in the company: customers, jobs, invoices, files. There's one, in the app shell; pages don't add their own (Global search, I10).
  • Search as a button on a toolbar that sits on the page rather than in a card, where search is used less than the filters beside it (Tasks, the pipeline). Trying out.
  • Inside a picker, a long list of options gets a find box over it: a plain Field, not this one.

One search design per place, app-wide: the audit found four.

Anatomy

  1. Field. A <label> around everything, so a click anywhere in it focuses the input. No edge at rest; a fill under the pointer (shown); the focus ring around the whole field.
  2. Magnifier. Secondary ink, 16px tall.
  3. Input. 14px, “Search” as its placeholder. It takes the room left in the row. Holding words, the browser's own clear (×) shows under the pointer or focus, redrawn in the system's ink.

Three places

In a toolbar

The table card's header, the to-do's and the calendar's bars. Bare, because the toolbar is already a frame: an edge around the field would box it twice. It sits between Add filter and the date range, and grows to fill the row.

Every state, held in place, is in the live specimen at the end of this page.

In the lists, what's typed narrows the rows as you type; Enter turns it into a chip beside the filters, reading Search: “Lakeside”, so a second search can follow it and both must match. A search chip opens its words again to edit them (Filter chip). A search is disabled while the list is still loading.

On the top bar

The app shell's one field, on the dark bar in both themes, so it takes the dark theme's colours. It reads as a well in the bar: a faint fill, a faint edge and a lighter line along its top, a step brighter under the pointer. Focused, it brightens again inside a 1px ring of the focus colour with a soft halo; its caret and selected words are Bolster's yellow. Trying out (D112).

Its shortcut sits at its end as keycaps, each with a pressed lower edge and a 4px corner (D42): ⌘ K at rest (Ctrl K off a Mac), esc while it's focused, and a Clear button (×) in their place once it holds words. The keycaps sit on the field's padding, clear of its corner; Clear sits inside it, its corner the field's less the gap around it (D35).

  • ⌘K, Ctrl+K or / from anywhere focuses it, its words selected.
  • Escape empties it, then leaves it.
  • Typing opens the results panel under it (Global search). On phones there's no top bar: search stays in the menu.

As a button, on a page's toolbar

Where a toolbar sits on the page and not in a card (Tasks, the pipeline), search is an icon button at the bar's end. Pressed, the field opens out of it, one control: it grows leftwards from the button's place to 14rem, the magnifier riding its edge, and shrinks back the same way. The button stays in the bar over the field's end, so nothing beside it moves. Trying out (D103, D104).

  • It stays open while it holds words, and closes when it's left empty.
  • Escape empties it, closes it and puts focus back on the button; the page searches again with nothing.
  • Open, it has an edge and the white surface, since there's no card around it. On phones the open field takes what the bar has left.
  • The behaviour is the shell's, written once (BolsterShell.searchButton); a page passes only what clearing does.

Usage

Content

  • Placeholder: “Search”, in the toolbar and the top bar alike (D130).
  • What it searches is said once, in the accessible name and the tooltip: “Search jobs. Search by client, job, address or number” (D46).
  • Search chip: “Search:” and the words in quotes.

Accessibility

  • type="search", with enterkeyhint="search" for the phone's keyboard and autocomplete="off".
  • There's no visible label, so the input takes an aria-label naming what it searches. The toolbar around it is a role="search" landmark.
  • The focus ring goes around the whole field, not the bare input, so it reads as one control.
  • The search button is icon-only: aria-label="Search", a tooltip, aria-controls pointing at the input and aria-expanded while it's open.
  • The keycaps are aria-hidden: they show a shortcut, they aren't controls. Clear is a real button with an aria-label.
